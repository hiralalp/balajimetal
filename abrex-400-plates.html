Abrex 400 Plates (400 BHN)

Abrex 400 is a wear-resistant steel plate with a nominal hardness of about 400 on the Brinell scale, which is why it is often listed as Abrex 400 BHN or 400 HBW. We supply Abrex 400 plates for parts that face abrasion, sliding contact and moderate impact.

The plate gets its hardness from quenching and tempering. Its surface is roughly two to three times as hard as ordinary structural mild steel, which slows the wear caused by rock, sand, coal, ore and clinker. A part made from it stays in service longer, so there are fewer plate changes and less downtime.

Grade 400 is the usual starting point among wear plates because it balances hardness against workability. It is hard enough for many abrasive jobs, yet it is easier to cut, bend and weld than the 500 and 600 grades. That makes it a practical choice for fabricated parts such as dumper bodies, hoppers and bucket shells.

Its high strength can also help reduce weight. Its yield strength is several times that of mild steel, so a designer may be able to use a thinner plate on a body or container and carry more payload. Any such change should be checked by the equipment designer, because stiffness and impact loads also matter.

This plate is not the right answer for every job. If sliding wear is very severe, a 450 or 500 grade may last longer. If a part is mainly loaded by heavy impact, a tougher grade may suit it better. Tell us what the plate handles and how it wears today, and we can help you compare options.

When you enquire, please send the thickness, plate size, quantity and any cutting drawing. Abrex is a brand name of its owner, and availability of sizes can vary, so we will confirm stock and mill documents with our quotation.

Fabrication Guide

General guidance only. Follow the plate maker's instructions for the exact thickness.

OperationGood practice
CuttingLaser, plasma and water jet are good on thinner plate. On oxy-fuel, control the speed and preheat thick plate to limit edge softening and cracking.
BendingUse a larger radius than for mild steel, bend across the rolling direction where possible, and allow for extra springback.
WeldingUse low-hydrogen consumables on a clean, dry joint, and control heat input.
PreheatingOften needed as thickness increases. Check the maker's chart.
DrillingUse a rigid machine, carbide or cobalt tooling, low speed and coolant.
Service temperatureHardness can fall if the plate is held at high temperature, generally above about 200 to 250 degrees C.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 400 BHN mean?

BHN stands for Brinell hardness number. A 400 BHN plate has a nominal surface hardness of about 400 on that scale. It is the same idea as 400 HBW.

Are Abrex 400 and Abrex 400 BHN different products?

No. Both names refer to the same grade. The second simply states the hardness.

Can Abrex 400 be welded?

Yes, using a suitable low-hydrogen procedure. Follow the maker's guidance, particularly on thick plate.
