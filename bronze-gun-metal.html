Bronze & Gun Metal

Bronzes are copper alloys in which tin, aluminium or other elements add strength and wear resistance. We supply phosphor bronze, aluminium bronze and gun metal, along with bronze rods in all sizes up to 160 mm diameter, and bush rounds in AB1 and AB2 aluminium bronze.

These alloys are valued for parts that slide, carry load or face corrosive water. Bronze runs well against steel with little risk of seizing, it resists corrosion, and it is easy to machine. That is why it is so widely used for bushes, bearings, gears, valve parts and marine fittings.

Phosphor bronze has tin and a small amount of phosphorus. It has good strength, wear resistance and spring properties, and it is common in bearings, gears, springs and electrical contacts. Higher tin grades offer better wear resistance, while lower tin grades are easier to form.

Aluminium bronze has the highest strength of this group. Grades AB1 and AB2 are widely used in marine and heavy-duty parts because they resist seawater corrosion and wear. AB2 contains added iron and nickel for greater strength.

Gun metal is a traditional copper-tin-zinc alloy, usually with a little lead in the leaded grades. It casts well and resists corrosion in water and steam, so it remains common in valves, pump parts, fittings and bushes. Its name comes from its original use in cannons.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is gun metal?

It is a copper alloy with tin and zinc, sometimes with lead, commonly used for valves, fittings and bushes.

What do AB1 and AB2 mean?

They are aluminium bronze grades. AB2 contains iron and nickel for higher strength.

Which alloy should I use for a bearing bush?

That depends on load, speed and lubrication. Phosphor bronze and leaded gun metal are common, and aluminium bronze suits heavy loads.
