Copper Coils & Tubes

Copper tubes and coils carry water, refrigerant, gas and process fluids and also serve in heat exchange. We supply tubes in copper, brass and cupronickel from 2 mm OD to 200 mm OD with wall thickness from 0.10 mm to 15 mm, in straight lengths up to 10 metres and in coils up to 25 metres.

Copper conducts heat very well, resists corrosion in many waters and atmospheres, and bends and brazes easily. These qualities make it the common choice for air conditioning and refrigeration, heat exchangers, plumbing, medical gas and instrument lines.

Coils and straight lengths each have their place. Soft annealed coils can be bent on site without many joints, which suits long runs and refrigeration lines. Hard drawn straight tubes hold their shape and need less support, so they suit exposed pipework and exchanger bundles. Half hard tubes sit between the two.

Brass and cupronickel extend the range. Admiralty and aluminium brass are used in condensers and heat exchangers. Cupronickel 90/10 and 70/30 are preferred for seawater because they resist corrosion and biofouling, and they are commonly used in shipbuilding, desalination and offshore systems.

DHP copper is the normal grade for tubes because its low residual phosphorus makes it easier to braze and weld without the embrittlement that can affect oxygen-bearing copper. Where the highest conductivity is needed, ETP or OFHC copper can be considered.

When you enquire, please give the alloy, OD, wall, temper, length or coil size and quantity. We can also supply copper strips and sections as per your requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is DHP copper?

It is phosphorus-deoxidised copper, the usual grade for tubes because it brazes and welds well.

Which should I use, a coil or a straight tube?

Coils suit long runs that need site bending. Straight hard tubes suit neat and exposed pipework.

Which alloy is best for seawater?

Cupronickel 90/10 and 70/30 are the common choices.
