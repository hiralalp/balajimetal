MS Sheets & Plates

Mild steel sheets and plates are flat rolled products used across fabrication, construction and pressure equipment. We supply carbon steel and boiler quality plates to IS 2062 Grade A, B and C, IS 2002 Grade 1 and 2, ASTM A516 Grade 60 and 70, and ASTM A515 Grade 70.

The difference between sheet and plate is mainly thickness. Thin material, generally under about 5 mm, is called sheet, and heavier material is called plate. Sheets go into panels, ducts, enclosures and pressed parts. Plates go into structures, machine bases, tanks and vessels.

Hot rolled material is the standard product. It is economical and suited to welding and fabrication, with a blue-grey mill scale on its surface. Cold rolled sheet is rolled further at room temperature, which gives it a smoother surface, closer thickness and better appearance, and it is used for parts that will be painted or pressed.

Structural plates and boiler quality plates are not the same. IS 2062 is used for general structures. IS 2002, ASTM A516 and ASTM A515 are made for boilers and pressure vessels and have controlled chemistry and properties. A516 is known for good notch toughness at moderate and lower temperatures, and A515 is used for higher temperature service.

Grade choice should follow the design code. Where a part carries pressure, the grade, thickness and test certificate should match the code requirements. For structural work, E250 is the common starting point, and higher strength grades can reduce weight.

When you enquire, please give the grade, thickness, width, length, quantity and any test or inspection requirements. Plates can also be profile cut to your drawing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is IS 2062?

It is the Indian standard for hot rolled structural steel, commonly used for general fabrication and structures.

What is the difference between A516 and A515?

Both are pressure vessel steels. A516 is commonly used for moderate and lower temperatures, and A515 for higher temperatures.

Can you cut plates to shape?

Yes. Please see our MS Profile Cutting page for cutting methods and tolerances.

How is plate weight calculated?

Weight in kg = length (m) x width (m) x thickness (mm) x 7.85.
