Perforated Sheets & Coils

Perforated sheets and coils are flat metal products punched with a regular pattern of holes. We supply stainless steel perforated sheet and coil from 0.4 mm to 10 mm thick and 100 mm to 1500 mm wide, in standard lengths from 2000 mm to 6000 mm or in coil form.

Perforating changes how a sheet behaves. It lets air, liquid, light and sound pass through while the remaining metal keeps the panel rigid. The same sheet can act as a filter, a screen, a guard, a sound-absorbing face or a decorative panel, and it weighs less than solid sheet of the same thickness.

The pattern is described by three things: the shape of the hole, its size, and the pitch, which is the centre-to-centre distance between holes. Together they decide the open area, the percentage of the sheet that is hole. A higher open area gives better flow and lower weight. A lower open area keeps more strength and stiffness.

Round holes in a staggered pitch are the most common choice because they pack the most holes into an area while keeping the sheet strong. Capsule (slotted) holes give a larger open area for the same web and suit grading, drying and drainage. Square and hexagonal holes give a high open area and a distinctive look for architectural work.

The grade is chosen for the environment. 304 suits most indoor, food and general industrial work. 316 and 316L are preferred near chlorides, chemicals and coastal air. 310S and 321 are used at higher temperatures, and 410, 430 and 409 are chosen where cost matters more than maximum corrosion resistance.

When you enquire, please tell us the grade, thickness, sheet size, hole shape, hole size, pitch and surface finish. If you only know the job, such as the flow rate or the particle size to retain, tell us that and we can help work out a suitable pattern.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is open area?

It is the percentage of the sheet taken up by holes. A 40% open sheet has 40% hole and 60% metal.

Does perforating reduce strength?

Yes. The more metal removed, the lower the strength and stiffness. A thicker sheet or lower open area can make up for it.

Can the hole be smaller than the sheet thickness?

As a general rule the hole should be at least equal to the sheet thickness. Smaller holes in thick sheet are harder to punch, so please check with us.

Can I get solid margins around the holes?

Yes. Plain margins and creative edgings can be arranged. Tell us the margin size on each side.
