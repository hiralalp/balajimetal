SS Weld Mesh

Weld mesh is a grid of wires joined by resistance welding at every crossing point. We supply stainless steel weld mesh in widths from 100 mm to 1500 mm and lengths of 2000 mm, 2440 mm, 3000 mm, 5800 mm and 6000 mm, as well as in coil form.

Because every joint is welded, weld mesh holds its shape. The wires do not slide when the mesh is cut, handled or loaded, and the panels stay flat and square. That makes it a good choice for guards, cages, partitions and panels where the opening must stay the same size.

Weld mesh differs from woven mesh, which is made by interlacing wires on a loom. Woven mesh can be made much finer and is the usual choice for filtration. Weld mesh is the stronger and more rigid option for structural and safety work at larger openings.

A mesh is described by its aperture, which is the clear gap between wires, and its wire diameter. Mesh count is the number of openings per inch. A bigger aperture gives better visibility and flow, while a thicker wire gives greater load capacity. Open area and weight both follow from those two figures, as shown in the table on this page.

Stainless steel mesh is used where mild or galvanised mesh would rust, or where it must stay clean. Grade 304 suits most indoor and food-related uses. Grade 316 is better for coastal, chemical or wet conditions.

When you enquire, tell us the grade, wire diameter, aperture, panel size and quantity. We can also supply from your drawing if you need special sizes or cut panels.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is weld mesh the same as wire mesh?

Wire mesh is the general term. Weld mesh is one type, joined by welding. Woven mesh is another type.

How is aperture measured?

It is the clear gap between two neighbouring wires, not the distance between wire centres.

Can you cut panels to size?

Yes. Send the panel sizes and the quantity of each.

Is weld mesh good for filtration?

It is generally used at larger openings. Fine filtration normally uses woven mesh, sometimes supported by a stronger mesh or perforated plate.
